The outlier

The scale page draws every package in the register at one rate. A pixel is US$10,000, and it is still US$10,000 at the far end. That fixed rate is the only reason the page means anything.

One entry cannot be drawn at it. At US$10,000 a pixel the Tesla package is 100,000,000 pixels long, and browsers give out a long way short of that. So it sits on the register uncounted and undrawn, and this page is the hole it left.

Below, the register stands in a corridor. The largest package is nearest, the smallest is at the vanishing point, and every entry is in there somewhere. Depth is only the order they come in. Width is the money. Pull back until the Tesla bar fits inside the frame, and watch what the same move does to the other hundred and fifty.

The drawing needs JavaScript. The numbers below do not.

At the scale page’s rate the register — every entry on it, 1989 to now — is 315 metres of bar. The Tesla package is 26.5 kilometres.

It is 84 times the whole register, and 909 times the longest bar the scale page draws.

That figure is a promise, and pays in full only at an US$8.5tn market capitalisation. What Musk realised in 2025 is not a promise: the AFL-CIO puts it at US$158.3bn — 4.2 kilometres, 13 times the register, and 2.5 million times the pay of the median Tesla worker.

The entry is on the register, counted in neither the total nor the scale, and saying so.
